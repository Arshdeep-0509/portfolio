"Website or app?" comes up in almost every first conversation with a founder, and it usually comes up
too soon. The answer has much less to do with technology than with who your users are, how they'll
discover you, how often they'll return and what you can afford to look after once version one is out.

In brief: if people will reach you through search or shared links, use you on a work laptop, or only
need you now and then, begin with a web app — Next.js is my usual pick. One codebase, instant
updates, and every page can be found on Google. If the product belongs in people's pockets — a daily
habit, notifications, the camera, location, offline use — a phone app starts to justify its extra
cost. At that point the choice is React Native or Flutter.

Both are mature, both target Android and iOS from one codebase, and both can feel properly native.
React Native shares its language and much of its thinking with a React or Next.js site, which helps
if you'll run both. Flutter renders very consistently across devices and suits teams that don't
already work in JavaScript. For most first versions either will serve; your team, your web plans and
the particular features you need decide it.

There's a fourth route founders often miss: launch a quick, phone-first web app, prove the idea, and
add a native app once you know people come back every day. On budget phones and weak networks, a
well-built web app can feel faster than an app people must download first.

Below I compare the routes on what really matters for a first product, and give you a decision path
you can work through in an afternoon.

```txt
Will people reach you through search or shared links?
  └─ yes → begin with a web app (Next.js)
  └─ no  → Are daily use, push, camera or offline essential?
            └─ yes → phone app (React Native or Flutter)
            └─ no  → phone-first web app, add an app later
```
