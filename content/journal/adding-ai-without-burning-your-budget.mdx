Nearly every founder I talk with feels some pull to "do something with AI". The push may come from
investors, from rivals, or from a genuinely good idea. Whatever the source, the danger is the same:
weeks of effort and a surprisingly large bill for a feature that demos nicely, sounds certain, and is
wrong just often enough that nobody relies on it.

The AI features that earn their keep tend to have three things in common. They aim at a narrow,
repetitive job with a clear correct answer — common support questions, fields on an invoice, the
right paragraph in a stack of documents. They draw on your own data rather than the model's general
knowledge. And they're measured before release against real cases from your business, so you know
how often they're right instead of hoping.

Spending is the other half. Models charge per token, which is easy to overlook during testing and
easy to be startled by in production. Fortunately most of that cost is in your hands: use a smaller
model where it's good enough, send less text, cache repeated work, and set firm limits before real
users show up.

This post covers how I approach it: finding a task worth automating, building a cheap prototype in
a week or two, scoring it with a test set, and the few engineering habits that keep a monthly AI
invoice dull. You don't need a research team — only the discipline you'd give any other feature.

A small check in code helps as well:

```ts
// Turn away requests that would take today's spend past the daily budget.
export async function withinBudget(estimatedCost: number) {
  const spentToday = await getSpendForToday(); // from your usage log
  return spentToday + estimatedCost <= DAILY_AI_BUDGET;
}
```
